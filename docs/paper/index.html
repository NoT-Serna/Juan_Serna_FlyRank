CTR / Engagement Opportunity Scoring: A Time-Aware Content Review Queue

Juan Serna · FlyRank ML Internship, Capstone · Lane 4 · 2026

Abstract

  1. Question. Which content pages should a marketing or UX team review first for CTR optimization, and can a simple model prioritize them usefully?
  2. Method. I framed this as binary classification: needs_review = 1 when a page's CTR is below the CTR expected for its position bucket and it has at least 100 impressions. A logistic regression on impressions, avg_position and ctr was trained on April 2025 features against May outcomes.
  3. Result. Tested on May features against June outcomes, the model measured F1 = 0.716 (precision 0.61, recall 0.87), against 0.709 under a random split of the training months.
  4. Interpretation. The score did not fall on future data, so the feature–label relationship looks stable across this three-month window. It is only modestly above a trivial "flag everything" predictor (F1 ≈ 0.67).
  5. Deliverable. A ranked review queue with reason codes and a leakage audit, offered as directional decision support for human triage, not as causal evidence about SEO outcomes.

1. Introduction

Content teams own far more pages than they can inspect each month. Most sit in a crowded middle: not clearly broken, not clearly thriving. Choosing which pages to look at first is an operational bottleneck that shapes how marketing and UX time is spent.

The decision this work supports is a weekly triage: of the pages we own, which should a reviewer check for title, snippet or intent-alignment problems? A ranked queue answers it.

A fixed rule is transparent but binary. It cannot order the flagged pages. A model that outputs a probability can. A false positive costs a reviewer some time, and a false negative leaves a fixable page unexamined for another month. Both costs are small, which is why the output is a priority ordering and not an automated action.

Scope: one three-month window (April–June 2025), one model family, one rule-defined label.

3. Methodology

Framing

Binary classification. One row is one client–content pair aggregated to a calendar month. The success metric is F1 on the positive class, measured on a future month.

Label definition

A page gets needs_review = 1 in a given month when both hold:

  1. ctr_gap > 0: its CTR is below the expected CTR for its average-position bucket.
  2. impressions ≥ 100: there is enough volume for CTR to be informative.
Position bucket1–33–55–1010–2020+
Expected CTR1.95%1.83%0.85%0.69%0.22%

The benchmarks are mean CTR per bucket observed in April 2025. The label is a defined rule, not an observed business outcome. The model learns to anticipate that rule one month ahead.

Features, model and baselines

Three features from the feature month only: impressions, avg_position, ctr. The derived columns expected_ctr, ctr_gap, position_bucket and needs_review were kept out of the feature set.

Pipeline(
  SimpleImputer(strategy="median"),
  StandardScaler(),
  LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000, random_state=42)
)

Logistic regression was chosen as an interpretable, honest baseline before more complex models. Two reference points frame its score: a trivial predictor that flags every page, and the Week 4 rule applied to May features to predict June.

Leakage checks

Seven checks were run and all passed: no label-derived features, target absent from X, temporal order preserved, preprocessing fitted inside the pipeline, no ID features, no future target in training, and identical feature sets for train and test. These checks are partly structural, since they confirm what the code does and not what the data contain. One real caveat remains: the same pages appear in consecutive months, so the time-aware split is not grouped by client or page.

4. Results

On the time-aware test (May features → June outcomes) the logistic model measured an F1 of 0.716. The random split gave 0.709, a difference of 0.007, so there is no sign of temporal degradation over this horizon.

Where the model is right and wrong

Under the time-aware split the model is strongly tilted toward flagging pages: it finds 87% of pages that later meet the rule but is right about only 61% of the pages it flags.

The queue

Pages are sorted by predicted probability of needing review. Figures 3 and 4 are exported by the notebook from an April 2025 snapshot.

Among the top-ranked pages, predicted probabilities are effectively 1.0, so order within that group reflects tiny numeric differences. Treat the top of the queue as a group to review, not as a precise ranking.

5. Limitations and honest framing

What this work can claim

  • Observed: over April–June 2025, a logistic regression on three features anticipated the rule-defined label with F1 ≈ 0.72 on future data.
  • Directional: higher-ranked pages are more likely to satisfy the review rule next month.
  • Decision support: the output is a prioritization list for human triage.

What it cannot claim

  • That editing a flagged page improves its CTR. No intervention was run.
  • Anything about how a search engine ranks pages. Positions and CTRs are inputs, not a model of ranking.
  • That the result holds beyond this window. It was measured on a single horizon.
  • Anything about engagement beyond clicks. Only Search Console signals were used.

Methodological caveats

  • The label is a rule, and two of the three features (ctr, avg_position) are the rule's own inputs. The model largely learns to reproduce the rule one month ahead, so the F1 reflects month-to-month stability of page performance as much as model skill.
  • The margin over flagging every page is modest (0.716 against 0.673), and the model leans toward over-flagging.
  • Pages recur across months and clients, so the time-aware split is not grouped by client or page.
  • Expected-CTR benchmarks come from April data, the same month used for training features.

6. Ranked recommendations: action playbook

The deliverable is work/outputs/action_queue.csv: one row per client–content pair, sorted by review_probability, with a reason code and a recommended human action.

Reason codeMeaningRecommended action
LOW_CTR_FOR_POSITIONCTR is below the benchmark for its position bucketReview title, snippet and search-intent alignment
LOW_VOLUMEFewer than 100 impressionsMonitor and gather more evidence before changing content
MODEL_FLAG_REVIEWThe model flags the page but the rule does notHuman investigation
NO_IMMEDIATE_ACTIONNot prioritizedNo immediate review

Intended use

Marketing and UX leads running weekly triage open the queue, read the top rows, and assign reviewers to items marked LOW_CTR_FOR_POSITION or MODEL_FLAG_REVIEW. It should not drive automated edits, deletions, redirects or rewrites.

Before acting on a row

  1. Search intent: does the page satisfy the intent of the queries it ranks for?
  2. Evidence volume: are there enough impressions to act, or only to monitor?
  3. Business context: is the page deliberately conservative for compliance, brand or legal reasons?

Monitoring and retraining

Re-run the time-aware evaluation monthly. Consider retraining when:

  • F1 falls more than 0.05 below 0.716 for two consecutive months.
  • The share of needs_review = 1 moves more than 10 percentage points from the training window.
  • Feature distributions shift, for example median position moving by a bucket.
  • The top of the queue stops capturing later needs_review cases.
  • The label definition or the expected-CTR benchmarks change.
